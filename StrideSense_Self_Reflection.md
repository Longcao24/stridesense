# StrideSense — Presentation Self-Reflection

Overall the talk did what we wanted: we opened with a concrete pain point, supported the claim with a live demo rather than slides alone, and closed with a specific ask. People could see the product was real, not just a concept. Below is our reflection on the four focus areas and what we would change.

## 1. The problem
We framed it clearly — at the foot, left–right asymmetry, impact overload, and unstable landings are invisible to the eye, so athletes train by feel and end up with injury and performance plateaus. The framing resonated. Next time we would anchor it to a sharper, *named* customer (e.g., an ACL/ankle return-to-sport cohort) with a number or short story, so the "why now" and market size are felt immediately rather than implied.

## 2. Our proposed solution
A removable foot-mounted 6-axis IMU pod embedded in Fitasy's custom 3D-printed shoes, reconstructing trajectory and full gait for both athletic performance and sport rehab. Our strongest differentiator was the integration story — the custom shoe is exactly what makes a seamless, unnoticeable pod possible. The improvement: we leaned a bit too far into engineering detail (two hardware routes, sensor specs). For a partner audience we should lead with the integrated user experience and outcome, and keep parts/specs as backup.

## 3. Current validation status
We were deliberately honest: the phone demo proves feasibility — one iPhone already reconstructs single-foot gait with per-step drift correction (ZUPT) — but it is a single short walk, uncalibrated, and not yet benchmarked against motion-capture ground truth. We mapped 11 gait features into 7 "absolute" and 4 "relative." This honesty built credibility but also exposed our biggest gap: no benchmarked accuracy. Next time we would bring even a small validation against a reference system (Vicon/Plantiga). We also re-learned that a live demo is fragile — an idle browser tab nearly cost us a sensor-permission failure — so we will always carry a pre-recorded video and screenshots as a Plan B.

## 4. Potential partnership, deployment, or commercialization
Our ask was concrete: one joint real-shoe pilot using an off-the-shelf module in a custom midsole pocket, on verifiable milestones, within a B2B2C model. To make it easier to say yes, next time we would arrive with the pilot fully specified — timeline, number of pairs, cost split, success metrics, and clear terms on data ownership and revenue share — so we are positioned as a software/IP partner rather than a parts vendor.

## Top three improvements
1. Sharpen the problem to a named customer with data.
2. Lead with outcome and integration, not specs.
3. Bring benchmarked validation plus a demo fallback.
